SafeCircle's AI is an assistive signal. This page lists what it cannot do and where it is known to be weak, so you can set expectations with your family.

<Callout type="warn" title="Not a substitute for talking to your child">
  The model can miss serious conversations and can flag harmless ones. Do not rely on it as the only safeguard.
</Callout>

## Coverage

- **Android only, five apps.** The child app reads text from WhatsApp, Instagram, Telegram, Snapchat and TikTok. Other apps, websites and games are not read.
- **Text on screen only.** Images, voice messages, video, and anything an app does not expose to Android's Accessibility API are not analysed.
- **A sample, not a full read.** Each app is analysed at most once every 5 minutes, using the last 10 visible text snippets. A short risky exchange between checks can be missed.
- **Basic by default.** A fresh install uses the keyword scan until you choose an AI model tier. A model tier analyses nothing until its download has finished and the model has loaded.
- **Failures are silent.** If the model fails to load, crashes or returns an unreadable answer, the app treats it as "no risk" and sends nothing.

## Accuracy

- **Trained and tested on AI-generated text.** The training data is synthetic. Every number we have comes from AI-written test chats. **Accuracy on real children's conversations has not been measured.**
- **Strong on familiar text, weaker on new text.** On held-out data from the training generator, category scores are near-perfect. On chats written by two other AI models, the current Horizon Mobile build caught about 63 to 68% of risky chats with the right category and flagged about 20% of harmless ones. Recall for `threats` and `grooming` fell the most. See [Horizon models](/en/docs/ai/horizon).
- **False positives are expected.** Banter between friends, role-play and game talk can look like risk. This is why medium alerts are labelled as worth a look, not as findings.
- **Severity is the hardest part.** In the internal evaluation, distinguishing `high` from `critical` was the weakest area. The child app asks for three levels (`low`, `medium`, `high`) and treats anything else as `low`.
- **Confidence is not a probability.** It is not calibrated. See [Risk categories](/en/docs/ai/risk-categories#confidence).
- **Phone accuracy is unmeasured.** The on-device builds were measured for speed and memory on real phones, but classification accuracy was measured on servers.
- **The keyword scan is crude.** It uses fixed words and phrases, so it misses rephrasing and some spellings and can fire on innocent text.

## Languages

- The training data targets **English and Spanish** (about 60% and 40%).
- There are no published per-language accuracy figures for Horizon.
- Other languages are not part of the training data and should be assumed unsupported.
- In the cross-generator tests, Spanish conversations were a weak spot for the Vigil research model in earlier rounds, and the gap narrowed after training on more varied data.

## What an alert does not tell you

- It does not quote or summarise the conversation. The note on an alert is a short summary of the categories found.
- It does not say who the other person is, or whether they are an adult.
- It cannot tell you whether something is a one-off or a pattern. Look at the [Timeline](/en/docs/platform/dashboard/analytics-timeline) for repeated alerts.

## Known gaps in the research

- No human-written test set exists yet.
- Neither Horizon nor the Vigil research model reaches the team's 2% false-alarm target on text it has not seen.
- Real-phone battery and heat impact has not been measured.
- Earlier recorded grooming-recall figures for Horizon could not be reproduced in a later benchmark.

## What helps

- Treat alerts as conversation starters. Ask about what happened with curiosity.
- Keep the child app and the model up to date. Updates are checked once a day.
- If alerts are missing or seem wrong, work through the [FAQ and troubleshooting](/en/docs/support/faq).
